## Corrections, retractions, and later challenges

On 2026-11-12, checked exact DOI queries in Europe PMC core metadata for the seven indexed original sources, plus DOI records in Crossref for all eight original sources. Europe PMC returned no match for S42, which was instead checked through its publisher identity and Crossref record. The S08 Crossref request initially returned HTTP 429 and succeeded on retry. Retrieved notice relationships contained comments, preprint versions, and review links, not an indexed erratum/correction/retraction notice. Public publisher pages were also inspected where accessible; searches used each DOI with “correction,” “erratum,” and “retraction.”

No correction/retraction notice was identified in those checks. **This is a dated search result, not proof that no notice or challenge exists.** Metadata deposit/coverage can be incomplete. “Comment in” is not “Erratum in”; the Basu adapter-processing literature is not an author correction. The three new sources' Europe PMC records likewise linked preprints rather than correction notices at the time retrieved.

Recent-literature searches covered DNA melting/model scope, cyclizability/adapter processing, NIPBL/cohesin depletion, and HP1 material behavior using 2025–2026 terms. The most relevant primary updates were S43–S45 and were read as above. Popay directly discusses the earlier ten-day mouse experiment and helps delimit its timing interpretation. Ke's study reinforces scope/implementation distinctions rather than invalidating all older parameters. Gordiychuk's paper keeps corrected assay data and MD validation distinct.

Additional leads were triaged without importing new public claims: Park et al., DOI 10.1101/2024.12.22.629997, and Brennan et al., DOI 10.1101/2024.10.18.618981. The retrieved PMC records identify preprints despite later indexing/availability dates; they must not be called peer-reviewed solely because of a 2025 date or PMC inclusion. A 2026 HP1α–DNA–lipid reconstitution paper (DOI 10.1002/smsc.70410) is another context lead, not evidence that the mouse-cell result is universally overturned. This was a focused update of current article claims, not an exhaustive review of chromatin or mechanics.
