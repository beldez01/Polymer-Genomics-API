# Chromosome 4

> *The chromosome of contradictions -- home to both the most dreaded single-gene disease in medicine and the largest gene deserts ever discovered in the human genome.*

## Physical Properties

- **Length**: ~190.2 Mb (GRCh38), representing 6.0-6.5% of total genomic DNA. The 4th largest human autosome.
- **Centromere type**: Submetacentric, with the centromere offset toward the short arm. Denver Group B (chromosomes 4-5).
- **Arm sizes**: Short arm (4p) ~48 Mb; long arm (4q) ~142 Mb, giving an arm ratio of approximately 3:1.
- **GC content**: 38.2%, one of the lowest among human autosomes, well below the genome-wide mean of ~41%.
- **Gene density**: ~3.8 protein-coding genes per Mb, ranking among the most gene-poor autosomes. Only 757-796 protein-coding genes confirmed.
- **Alu density**: 7.8% -- notably lower than GC-richer chromosomes, consistent with the known Alu-GC correlation.

## Genomic Architecture

- **Isochore structure**: Dominated by L1 and L2 (AT-rich) isochores, with only scattered H1 isochore islands. The paucity of GC-rich isochores explains both the low gene density and late replication timing.
- **Segmental duplications**: The 4p16 subtelomeric region shares ~99% sequence identity with chromosome 10q26, including the D4Z4 tandem repeat array. This paralogous duplication has clinical implications for FSHD diagnosis.
- **Gene deserts**: Contains what are believed to be the largest gene deserts yet discovered in the human genome, paradoxically among the most conserved noncoding sequences across mammals and birds.
- **Evolutionary breakpoints**: A pericentric inversion distinguishes human chromosome 4 from the chimpanzee homolog (PTR3), with breakpoints at HSA4p14 and 4q21.3.

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **HTT (Huntingtin)** (4p16.3): The gene behind Huntington disease, caused by CAG trinucleotide repeat expansion. Normal: 9-36 repeats; disease: 37+; juvenile onset: 55+. Mapped in 1983, cloned in 1993. The huntingtin protein is 3,144 amino acids (348 kDa), among the largest human proteins.
- **FGFR3** (4p16.3): Gain-of-function mutations cause achondroplasia (most common form of dwarfism, ~1:25,000), with a single G380R substitution responsible for >98% of cases.
- **WFS1** (4p16.1): Mutations cause Wolfram syndrome (DIDMOAD -- diabetes insipidus, diabetes mellitus, optic atrophy, deafness). 65% of patients die before age 35.
- **KIT** (4q12): Receptor tyrosine kinase for stem cell factor. Mutations cause piebaldism and GISTs. Adjacent to PDGFRA, forming a receptor tyrosine kinase mini-cluster.
- **ADH gene cluster** (4q23): Seven alcohol dehydrogenase genes in ~370 kb. The ADH4 enzyme variant arose ~10 million years ago, likely enabling ancestors to metabolize ethanol in fermenting fruit.
- **D4Z4/DUX4** (4q35): Tandem array of 3.3 kb repeat units at the subtelomere. Contraction to 1-10 repeats on a 4qA allele causes FSHD1 through de-repression of DUX4, a germline transcription factor that should be silenced in somatic tissues.
- **EVC/EVC2** (4p16.2): Adjacent genes whose mutations cause Ellis-van Creveld syndrome, notably prevalent in the Old Order Amish.

## Disease Associations

- **Huntington disease**: Autosomal dominant, progressive neurodegeneration. Inverse correlation between repeat length and age of onset. Anticipation during paternal transmission.
- **Wolf-Hirschhorn syndrome (4p-)**: Terminal deletion of 4p16.3, causing "Greek warrior helmet" facies, severe intellectual disability, growth delay, seizures. Critical region includes WHSC1/NSD2 and LETM1.
- **Achondroplasia**: FGFR3 G380R gain-of-function. Over 80% de novo, overwhelmingly paternal origin, frequency increasing with paternal age.
- **FSHD**: Third most common muscular dystrophy (~1:20,000). Requires both D4Z4 contraction AND the 4qA haplotype. Chromosome 10q26 contractions are non-pathogenic because 10qA lacks the polyadenylation signal for DUX4 mRNA.
- **GWAS hits**: 4q25 (PITX2, atrial fibrillation), 4q27 (IL-2/IL-21, autoimmune diseases).

## Evolutionary History

- **Ancestral chromosome**: Human arrangement represents the ancestral state. The pericentric inversion distinguishing HSA4 from chimpanzee chromosome 3 occurred in the chimpanzee lineage.
- **Deep synteny**: Portions correspond to ancestral mammalian chromosomes (MAM) 1, 3, and 7. MAM7 showed remarkable conservation, with 95% of its length unaffected by intrachromosomal rearrangements for 76 million years.
- **Gene desert conservation**: The massive gene deserts are conserved across mammals, birds, and fish, implying functional constraint on noncoding regions for >400 million years.

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **D4Z4 chromatin**: Each 3.3 kb repeat contains a GC-rich core with CpG islands, forming a polymorphic heterochromatin domain. In FSHD, array contraction leads to hypomethylation and loss of H3K9me3.
- **HTT repeat structures**: The CAG expansion adopts unusual non-B-DNA structures including slipped-strand DNA and hairpins, contributing to repeat instability during replication.
- **AT-rich curvature**: The AT-rich composition predicts higher intrinsic curvature genome-wide, consistent with the isochore-curvature anticorrelation.

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **D4Z4 methylation as disease switch**: One of the most striking examples of disease-relevant epigenetics. In healthy individuals, the array is heavily methylated (>50%) and coated in H3K9me3/H3K27me3. In FSHD, hypomethylation falls below 25%, enabling DUX4 expression.
- **Late replication timing**: Most of chromosome 4 replicates in mid-to-late S phase. The 4q35 subtelomeric region replicates particularly late.
- **Enhancer landscape**: Gene deserts contain ultraconserved enhancer elements regulating distant developmental genes.

## Deep Cuts

- **The Huntington paradox**: The normal huntingtin protein is essential -- complete knockout is embryonic lethal in mice. The mutant protein gains a toxic function rather than losing the normal one.
- **D4Z4 on chromosome 10**: Near-identical array (~99% identity) on 10q26 never causes FSHD. The sole difference is a single polymorphism in the pLAM region creating/destroying a polyadenylation signal.
- **The ADH evolutionary moment**: The ADH4 variant emergence ~10 Mya coincides with the transition to terrestrial lifestyles -- "the drunken monkey hypothesis."
- **Wolf-Hirschhorn and Huntington overlap**: WHS critical region encompasses HTT, but patients rarely survive long enough to develop Huntington symptoms.
- **Piebaldism patterns**: KIT mutations cause white patches revealing the embryonic neural crest migration pattern frozen in adult skin -- strikingly symmetrical because crest cells migrate in mirror-image streams.
- **LETM1 and epilepsy**: LETM1 haploinsufficiency directly causes seizures by disrupting mitochondrial calcium homeostasis -- one of the clearest examples of a mitochondrial gene causing epilepsy.
