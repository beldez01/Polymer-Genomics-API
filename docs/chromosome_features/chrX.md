# Chromosome X

> *"Every woman is a mosaic" -- the only chromosome where an entire copy is silenced, creating two genetically distinct cell populations in every female.*

## Physical Properties

- **Length**: ~155 Mb (~5% of the genome). First complete T2T assembly of any human chromosome (2020).
- **Centromere type**: Submetacentric, Group C.
- **Arm sizes**: Xp ~60 Mb, Xq ~95 Mb.
- **GC content**: ~39.5% -- below the genome average, making it one of the more AT-rich chromosomes.
- **Gene density**: Low at ~5.3 genes/Mb -- roughly half the genome average.
- **Protein-coding genes**: ~800-850.

## Genomic Architecture

- **LINE-1 enrichment**: The defining feature -- L1 elements cover ~29% (vs 17% genome average). L1-dense regions correlate with efficient X-inactivation; L1-poor regions harbor escape genes. Mary Lyon's "repeat hypothesis" (1998).
- **Five evolutionary strata**: Reflecting stepwise recombination suppression with Y: Stratum 1 (~240-320 MYA, oldest) through Stratum 5 (~30 MYA, youngest, adjacent to PAR1). Escape genes cluster on youngest strata.
- **PAR1** (2.6 Mb) and **PAR2** (320 kb): Pseudoautosomal regions still recombining with Y.
- **Fragile sites**: FRAXA at Xq27.3 (FMR1 CGG expansion) is the most clinically significant fragile site in the genome.

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **DMD/Dystrophin** (Xp21.2): Largest known human gene at 2.24 Mb -- 0.08% of the genome, 1.5% of chrX. Transcription takes ~16 hours. 79 exons, but mRNA only 14 kb (99.4% intronic). Mutations cause Duchenne/Becker muscular dystrophy.
- **FMR1** (Xq27.3): CGG repeat expansion >200 = methylation = silencing = Fragile X syndrome (most common inherited cause of intellectual disability, 1/4,000 males). Premutation (55-200) causes FXTAS in elderly males and FXPOI in females. CGG repeats form G-quadruplexes in both DNA and RNA.
- **XIST** (Xq13.2): 17 kb lncRNA that coats and silences one X chromosome. Located at the X-Inactivation Center. Its antisense partner TSIX regulates XIST on the active X.
- **G6PD** (Xq28): Most common enzyme deficiency in humans (400 million affected). Confers partial malaria resistance.
- **F8 and F9**: Factor VIII (Xq28) and IX (Xq27) -- Hemophilia A and B. The "Royal disease" from Queen Victoria.
- **Color vision genes** (Xq28): OPN1LW/OPN1MW tandem array. Unequal crossing-over causes red-green color blindness in ~8% of males.
- **AR** (Xq12): Androgen receptor. CAG repeat >36 causes Kennedy disease.
- **MECP2** (Xq28): Methyl-CpG-binding protein 2. Mutations cause Rett syndrome -- both an X-linked and an epigenetic disorder (MECP2 reads DNA methylation).

## Disease Associations

- **>500 X-linked conditions**: DMD, Hemophilia, G6PD deficiency, Fragile X, color blindness, Lesch-Nyhan, Hunter syndrome.
- **Turner syndrome (45,X)**: Only viable human monosomy. ~1:2,500 females.
- **Klinefelter syndrome (47,XXY)**: 1:600 males. 15-25% of genes that escape inactivation are overexpressed.
- **Rett syndrome**: MECP2 mutations. Almost exclusively affects females (lethal in hemizygous males).
- **Skewed X-inactivation**: When >80:20, female carriers of X-linked diseases can manifest symptoms. Skewing increases with age.

## Evolutionary History

- **Ohno's Law** (1967): X gene content conserved across all placental mammals -- validated across dog, cow, mouse (>95% shared).
- **Origin**: ~300 MYA from an ordinary autosomal pair when SOX3 mutated to SRY on the proto-Y.
- **Five strata**: Progressive Y inversions created five X-Y divergence strata over 300 MY.
- **LINE co-evolution**: L1 enrichment may have co-evolved with X-inactivation, providing repeat substrate for heterochromatin spreading.

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **Barr body**: Xi compacted to ~half the volume of Xa, at the nuclear periphery.
- **FMR1 G-quadruplex**: Extraordinarily stable G4 in both DNA and RNA. RNA G-quadruplexes directly pathogenic in FXTAS.
- **XIST RNA coating**: 17 kb RNA coats the Xi, recruiting PRC1/PRC2 for H2AK119ub and H3K27me3. Uses L1 elements as anchoring points.
- **Bipartite Xi structure**: Two mega-domains separated by DXZ4 macrosatellite, distinct from normal TAD architecture.
- **Replication timing**: Xi among the latest-replicating regions genome-wide.

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **X-inactivation cascade**: XIST coating -> PRC2 -> H3K27me3 -> DNA methylation -> deacetylation -> H2AK119ub -> macroH2A -> late replication. Extraordinarily stable once established.
- **Escape genes**: 15-25% escape inactivation, cluster on Xp, tend to have Y homologs. Major source of phenotypic sexual dimorphism.
- **DNA methylation dimorphism**: CpG islands on Xi heavily methylated (70-90%), same islands on Xa unmethylated (<10%).
- **Calico cats**: Classic visible manifestation of X-inactivation mosaicism.

## Deep Cuts

- **Every woman is a chimera**: Random X-inactivation makes every 46,XX female a mosaic of two cell populations expressing different X chromosomes.
- **The LINE hypothesis holds up**: 25+ years later -- X;autosome translocations silence autosomal genes only in L1-rich regions, and L1-depleted X regions correlate precisely with escape genes.
- **DMD transcription time**: ~16 hours to transcribe. Approaches the limits of what a mammalian cell cycle can accommodate.
- **First T2T chromosome**: ChrX was first complete gapless assembly (2020), partly because CHM13 had only one X and no Y.
- **G6PD and cancer monoclonality**: G6PD heterozygous females were used by Linder and Gartler (1965) to prove tumors are monoclonal -- foundational cancer biology enabled by X-inactivation.
- **XIST paradox**: XIST is expressed only from the inactive X -- the one "gene" whose activity marks the silent chromosome.
