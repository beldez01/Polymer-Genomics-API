# Chromosome 9

> *Home to the most deleted gene in cancer, the translocation that launched targeted therapy, the blood group that defined transfusion medicine, and the largest block of autosomal heterochromatin -- a chromosome of extremes.*

## Physical Properties

- **Length**: ~138 Mb total; 109 Mb of finished euchromatic sequence. ~4.0-4.5% of genomic DNA.
- **Centromere type**: Submetacentric. Denver Group C. Flanked by heterochromatic C-bands rich in satellite DNA.
- **Gene count**: ~800-900 protein-coding genes.
- **Heterochromatin**: Contains the largest autosomal block of constitutive heterochromatin (9q12/9qh), heteromorphic in 6-8% of the population.
- **Pericentromeric inversions**: inv(9)(p12q13) occurs in 1-2% of the population -- the most common constitutional chromosomal rearrangement. Generally benign.

## Genomic Architecture

- **Pericentromeric complexity**: 9qh region consists of satellite I, II, III, and beta-satellite DNA. Different inversion types have distinct satellite compositions.
- **Type I interferon cluster** (9p21-22): 26 interferon genes/pseudogenes including 16 IFN-alpha genes.
- **9p21.3 GWAS hotspot**: 59 SNPs with multiple long-range enhancers and lncRNAs. Most replicated genetic risk locus for cardiovascular disease AND associated with T2D and multiple cancers.

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **CDKN2A/CDKN2B** (9p21.3): A masterwork of genomic economy -- a single locus encoding two unrelated tumor suppressors (p16INK4a and p14ARF) through alternative reading frames. Second most commonly inactivated gene in cancer after TP53. Deleted in ~50% of melanomas and glioblastomas.
- **ABL1** (9q34.12): The t(9;22)(q34;q11) creates the Philadelphia chromosome -- the BCR-ABL1 fusion. Found in >90% of CML. Imatinib transformed CML from a death sentence into a manageable chronic disease.
- **ABO** (9q34.2): A and B alleles differ by 7 nucleotides (4 amino acid changes). O allele results from a single-nucleotide frameshift deletion -- the most common blood type worldwide from a broken gene. Type O has arisen independently at least three times. First human genetic polymorphism discovered (Landsteiner, 1901).
- **NOTCH1** (9q34.3): Activating mutations in >60% of T-ALL.
- **JAK2** (9p24.1): V617F in ~96% of polycythemia vera. Discovery unified classification of Ph-negative myeloproliferative neoplasms.
- **FXN** (9q21.11): GAA repeat expansion (66-1,700 vs normal 5-33) causes Friedreich ataxia, the most common inherited ataxia.
- **DMRT1** (9p24.3): Haploinsufficiency causes 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis -- one of few autosomal loci reversing sex determination. Homologs span >600 million years (dsx in Drosophila, mab-3 in C. elegans).
- **ANRIL/CDKN2B-AS1** (9p21.3): lncRNA with linear and circular isoforms mediating disease associations.

## Disease Associations

- **CML**: t(9;22) -- first consistent chromosomal abnormality linked to cancer (Nowell & Hungerford, 1960). The paradigm of targeted therapy.
- **Coronary artery disease**: 9p21.3 is the strongest replicated risk locus.
- **Cancer**: CDKN2A deletions across melanoma, glioblastoma, pancreatic, bladder, NSCLC, T-ALL.
- **Monosomy 9p (Alfi syndrome)**: Trigonocephaly, intellectual disability, and in 46,XY gonadal dysgenesis.
- **Friedreich ataxia**: Most common inherited ataxia.

## Evolutionary History

- **Ancient ohnologs**: MHC region (chr6) has ohnologs on chr1, 9, and 19, all descending from pre-whole-genome-duplication chromosome 9 of the ancestral vertebrate (>450 Mya).
- **ABO polymorphism age**: A and B alleles diverged before the human-chimpanzee split. Trans-species polymorphism maintained by balancing selection.
- **DMRT1 conservation**: Among the most ancient sex-determination genes, >600 million years of conservation.

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **9q12 heterochromatic block**: Largest on any autosome, consisting primarily of satellite II and III DNA. Can differ 3-fold in size between homologs in normal individuals. Replicates very late in S phase.
- **Late replication**: Pericentromeric satellite arrays are among the latest-replicating regions genome-wide.

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **9p21.3 nexus**: CDKN2A silenced by promoter hypermethylation in many cancers. ANRIL recruits Polycomb complexes (PRC1/PRC2) to regulate CDKN2B in cis. Circular ANRIL isoforms have distinct functions from linear forms.
- **IFN cluster chromatin**: Distinctive high-density nucleosome occupancy -- an epigenetic "safety lock" preventing inappropriate interferon activation.
- **Heterochromatin insulation**: Despite massive 9qh size variation, gene expression on euchromatic arms appears unaffected.

## Deep Cuts

- **The Philadelphia chromosome launched precision oncology**: The 41-year journey from cytogenetics (1960) to imatinib (2001) inaugurated molecularly targeted therapy.
- **9p21.3 is the most "multipurpose" risk locus**: Independently associated with CAD, T2D, melanoma, glioma, and more through distinct mechanisms. No other locus has so many disease associations.
- **The O blood type is a broken A**: The most common blood type worldwide results from a frameshift that destroys the entire protein, arising at least three times independently.
- **9qh heteromorphisms were once considered pathological**: Before molecular cytogenetics, enlarged 9qh blocks were incorrectly reported as abnormalities, causing parental anxiety.
- **Remarkable coincidence at 9q34**: ABL1, TSC1, NOTCH1, and ABO all reside within ~5 Mb at the distal tip of 9q -- the defining gene for blood transfusion, targeted therapy paradigm, a major signaling pathway, and a tumor suppressor.
- **The inv(9) is everywhere and does nothing**: Found in 1-2% of all humans, it stands as a monument to selectively neutral massive structural variation.
