# Chromosome 16

> *The duplication-rich chromosome: where segmental repeats breed structural chaos and the most common trisomy in human miscarriage hides in plain sight.*

## Physical Properties

- **Length**: ~90.3 Mb (78.9 Mb euchromatin + ~9.8 Mb centromeric heterochromatin). ~3% of total nuclear DNA.
- **Centromere type**: Metacentric to slightly submetacentric. Denver Group E (chromosomes 16-18).
- **Arm sizes**: p arm ~35.3 Mb, q arm ~54.9 Mb.
- **GC content**: ~44.7%, above the genome average. The 16p13.3 subtelomeric region is particularly GC-rich and Alu-rich.
- **Gene density**: ~1,100-1,150 protein-coding genes. Upper third of autosomes for gene density.
- **Segmental duplications**: ~9-10% of the chromosome, among the highest of any autosome.

## Genomic Architecture

- **Segmental duplication hotspot**: Enriched in the gene-poor pericentromere, with blocks of >98% identity mediating recurrent NAHR.
- **Fragile sites**: FRA16D (16q23.2, within WWOX) is the second most active common fragile site in the human genome. FRA16A at 16p13.11.
- **WWOX gene**: Spans >1.11 Mb with a massive 780-kb intron containing FRA16D. Functions as tumor suppressor and metabolic regulator.

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **Alpha-globin cluster** (16p13.3): HBA1 and HBA2 plus embryonic HBZ. The --SEA deletion is the most common cause of Hb Bart's hydrops fetalis. The HS-40 enhancer lies 40 kb upstream within an intron of the unrelated NPRL3 gene.
- **PKD1** (16p13.3): Encodes polycystin-1 (4,303 aa). ADPKD affects ~1:1,000 people. Six PKD1 pseudogenes complicate genetic testing.
- **TSC2** (16p13.3): Lies immediately adjacent to PKD1 in tail-to-tail orientation. Large deletions cause contiguous gene syndrome (polycystic kidneys + tuberous sclerosis).
- **VAC14** (16q24.3): Scaffold protein in the PIKfyve kinase complex. Pentamerizes into a star-shaped structure. Mutations cause childhood-onset neurodegeneration.
- **CBFB** (16q22): inv(16) fuses CBFB to MYH11 in ~10% of de novo AML (favorable prognosis M4Eo).
- **CDH1** (16q21-22): E-cadherin, classic tumor suppressor in lobular breast cancer and diffuse gastric cancer.
- **Metallothionein cluster** (16q13): 14 MT genes for heavy-metal binding and zinc/copper homeostasis.

## Disease Associations

- **Trisomy 16**: Most common autosomal trisomy in conceptions (~6% of all miscarriages, ~16% of first-trimester abortions). Full trisomy invariably lethal; only mosaics survive to birth.
- **16p11.2 deletion/duplication (~593 kb)**: One of the most common genetic causes of autism (~1% of ASD). Deletion: obesity (mean BMI +2 SD). Duplication: schizophrenia risk (14x), underweight.
- **ADPKD (PKD1)**: ~85% of ADPKD families. 12.5 million affected worldwide.
- **Alpha-thalassemia**: Most prevalent single-gene disorder globally (~5% carrier frequency).

## Evolutionary History

- **Synteny**: Mouse synteny distributed across chromosomes 7, 8, 16, and 17, indicating multiple fissions since the ~87 Mya ancestor.
- **Deep conservation**: Alpha-globin cluster syntenic with flanking genes for >500 My. In most non-primate mammals, alpha- and beta-globin are on the same chromosome; separation occurred in the primate ancestor.
- **Primate SD expansion**: Pericentromeric duplications underwent serial expansion during hominoid evolution.

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **Pericentromeric heterochromatin**: Satellite II/III DNA at 16q11 forms one of the most densely packed heterochromatic blocks in the genome.
- **FRA16D R-loops**: RNA:DNA hybrid forming sequences at FRA16D contribute to fragile site biology.
- **Centromere**: D16Z2 alpha satellite HOR fully resolved in T2T-CHM13.

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **CDH1 methylation**: Promoter hypermethylation is a hallmark of ~65% of lobular breast carcinomas.
- **Metallothionein regulation**: Dynamic methylation regulation in response to heavy metal exposure.
- **Replication timing**: GC-rich 16p13.3 replicates early; pericentromeric heterochromatin and FRA16D replicate late.

## Deep Cuts

- **Most common lethal trisomy**: Trisomy 16 is the single most frequent chromosome abnormality in miscarriages, yet no liveborns with the full form exist -- an "invisible killer."
- **PKD1 pseudogene confusion**: Six pseudogenes sharing ~97% identity with exons 1-33 made PKD1 one of the most difficult genes to sequence accurately for decades.
- **CBFB-MYH11 spatial paradox**: CBFB and MYH11 are ~50 Mb apart, yet form the inv(16) fusion -- farther apart than any other intrachromosomal rearrangement pair. Their 3D nuclear proximity in hematopoietic stem cells enables this.
- **Alpha-globin regulation mystery**: The critical HS-40 enhancer is buried inside an intron of NPRL3 -- a gene with nothing to do with hemoglobin.
- **The metabolic chromosome**: Disproportionate number of metabolic enzyme genes including GALT (galactosemia), ACADM, LCAT, and the metallothioneins.
