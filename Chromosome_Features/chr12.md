# Chromosome 12

> A chromosome of developmental control and oncogenic power -- home to the HOXC cluster that patterns the body, the KRAS oncogene that drives a quarter of all cancers, the keratin genes that armor epithelial surfaces, and NANOG that holds stem cells in their pluripotent state.

## Physical Properties

- **Total length**: ~133.3 Mb of finished sequence (2006 Nature paper); represents ~4.5% of the human genome
- **Centromere type/position**: Submetacentric; centromere at ~35.8 Mb, yielding a short arm (12p) of ~36 Mb and a long arm (12q) of ~97 Mb
- **Cytogenetic group**: Group C (medium-sized submetacentric chromosomes, along with chr 6--11 and X)
- **GC content**: ~40.8%, near the genome average. The 12q13 region (HOXC/keratin/CDK4 cluster) is notably GC-rich (H2/H3 isochore), while 12p12 (KRAS region) is GC-poor
- **Gene density**: ~10.7 genes/Mb, approximately average among autosomes (ranking ~12th--14th)
- **Gene count**: >1,400 protein-coding genes and 487 loci directly implicated in human disease

## Genomic Architecture

- **The 12q13 supercluster**: A ~5 Mb region at 12q13 is one of the most gene-dense and functionally concentrated segments in the human genome, containing:
  - The HOXC gene cluster (~100 kb, 9 genes)
  - The type II keratin gene cluster (~780 kb, 26 genes)
  - CDK4, CDK2, ERBB3, and other cell-cycle/signaling genes
  - VDR (vitamin D receptor)
  - This region is frequently amplified in sarcomas and is the core of the "12q13-15 amplicon"
- **Isochore structure**: Bimodal -- 12p is predominantly L1/L2 (GC-poor, gene-sparse), while 12q13 and distal 12q are H1/H2 (GC-rich, gene-dense). The compositional transition around the centromere is relatively abrupt
- **Segmental duplications**: Pericentromeric region of 12p contains segmental duplications shared with multiple chromosomes; subtelomeric regions of both arms participate in the genome-wide exchange network
- **Common fragile sites**: FRA12A (12q13.1) is a rare folate-sensitive fragile site associated with CGG repeat expansion
- **Repeat content**: LINE density is elevated in the gene-poor 12p arm; Alu elements are enriched in the gene-dense 12q13 region, following the genome-wide pattern of Alu-GC correlation

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **KRAS** (12p12.1) -- Kirsten Rat Sarcoma viral oncogene homolog. The most frequently mutated oncogene in human cancer: ~25% of all tumors harbor KRAS mutations, including ~90% of pancreatic cancers, ~35% of colorectal cancers, and ~30% of non-small cell lung cancers. KRAS G12C was the first "undruggable" target to be successfully drugged (sotorasib, approved 2021). Small GTPase acting as a molecular switch in RAS/MAPK signaling
- **HOXC cluster** (12q13.13) -- Nine homeobox transcription factors (HOXC4-HOXC13) arranged collinearly in ~100 kb. One of four HOX clusters (A, B, C, D on chromosomes 7, 17, 12, 2 respectively) that pattern the anterior-posterior body axis. HOXC genes specify thoracic and lumbar vertebral identity; HOXC13 is essential for hair shaft differentiation and nail formation; HOXC9 marks the thoracic-lumbar transition
- **CDK4** (12q14.1) -- Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 4. Spans only 4.16 kb (8 exons, 303 amino acids, 33.7 kDa protein). Phosphorylates Rb protein to drive G1/S transition. Amplified in liposarcoma (~90%), glioblastoma (~15%), and melanoma. Germline R24C mutation causes familial melanoma. CDK4/6 inhibitors (palbociclib, ribociclib, abemaciclib) have transformed HR+ breast cancer treatment
- **NANOG** (12p13.31) -- Pluripotency transcription factor spanning 8.3 kb. Named after Tir na nOg, the Celtic "Land of the Young." Master regulator maintaining embryonic stem cell self-renewal by suppressing differentiation programs. Reactivated in germ cell tumors and associated with cancer stemness
- **ETV6** (12p13.2) -- ETS Variant Transcription Factor 6. Forms the ETV6-RUNX1 (TEL-AML1) fusion via t(12;21)(p13;q22) -- the most common genetic alteration in pediatric ALL (~25% of cases), conferring favorable prognosis. ETV6 is a recurrent fusion partner in multiple malignancies
- **VDR** (12q13.11) -- Vitamin D Receptor. Nuclear receptor that mediates vitamin D's effects on calcium homeostasis, immune regulation, and cell differentiation. VDR polymorphisms (FokI, BsmI, TaqI, ApaI) are among the most studied genetic variants in epidemiology, with associations reported (and debated) for osteoporosis, autoimmune disease, and cancer risk
- **GAPDH** (12p13.31) -- Glyceraldehyde-3-Phosphate Dehydrogenase. The most widely used "housekeeping gene" reference in molecular biology. Located near NANOG on 12p. Despite its reputation for constitutive expression, GAPDH is now recognized as having oncogenic roles via moonlighting functions in apoptosis, DNA repair, and membrane fusion
- **Type II keratin gene cluster** (12q13.13) -- 26 type II keratin genes spanning ~780 kb (type I keratins are on chromosome 17). Mutations cause epidermolytic hyperkeratosis (K1/K10), monilethrix (hair keratins), and other genodermatoses
- **STAT6** (12q13.3) -- Signal transducer activated by IL-4/IL-13; master regulator of Th2 immune responses and allergic inflammation

## Disease Associations

- **Pallister-Killian syndrome**: Mosaic tetrasomy 12p -- extra isochromosome i(12p) present in a mosaic pattern. Features include coarse facies, pigmentary skin anomalies, seizures, intellectual disability, sparse temporal hair, and diaphragmatic hernia. The isochromosome is often undetectable in blood lymphocytes (only found in fibroblasts), making skin biopsy necessary for diagnosis. Prevalence <1/100,000
- **12q13-15 amplicon in sarcoma**: The most characteristic cytogenetic abnormality of well-differentiated liposarcoma (atypical lipomatous tumor). Amplification of MDM2, CDK4, and HMGA2 drives this tumor; the amplified region can exceed 100 Mb of total amplified DNA in ring and giant marker chromosomes
- **ETV6-RUNX1 in childhood ALL**: t(12;21)(p13;q22) present in ~25% of pediatric B-cell precursor ALL. Often initiates in utero (found in neonatal blood spots of children who develop ALL years later), but requires secondary hits. Associated with excellent prognosis (>90% cure rate)
- **Noonan syndrome**: Germline KRAS mutations (12p12.1) cause a subset of Noonan syndrome cases. Other genes in the RAS/MAPK pathway (PTPN11, RAF1, SOS1) account for most cases, but KRAS mutations produce a particularly severe phenotype
- **Trisomy 12 in CLL**: Trisomy 12 is found in ~15-20% of chronic lymphocytic leukemia cases and defines an intermediate-prognosis subgroup. It is associated with atypical morphology and unmutated IGHV status
- **Phenylketonuria**: The PAH gene (phenylalanine hydroxylase) at 12q23.2 is mutated in classical PKU, one of the most common inborn errors of metabolism (~1/10,000 births in European populations)
- **GWAS hotspots**: Type 2 diabetes (multiple loci), asthma (STAT6 region), inflammatory bowel disease, schizophrenia

## Evolutionary History

- **Ancestral origin**: Chromosome 12 derives from a double reciprocal rearrangement between two ancient acrocentric chromosomes containing material syntenic to modern chromosomes 12 and 22. This rearrangement occurred in the anthropoid lineage after the divergence of prosimians (~40 Mya)
- **Deep conservation**: Alignment with the chicken genome (>300 Myr divergence) identifies 13 segments covering ~72% of chromosome 12. The HOXC cluster is conserved across all bilaterians (>550 Myr), and the keratin gene cluster organization is shared across amniotes
- **Primate divergence**: While chromosome 12 has not undergone major subsequent rearrangements since the anthropoid ancestor, pericentric inversions have occurred independently in chimpanzee and gorilla orthologs, resulting in different banding patterns despite conserved gene content
- **Evolutionary new centromere**: The orangutan ortholog of chromosome 12 contains an evolutionary new centromere (ENC) -- an epigenetically specified centromere that repositioned without DNA rearrangement. This was the first clear evidence that centromere seeding is an epigenetic phenomenon, not dependent on specific DNA sequences
- **Rate of substitution**: The 2006 Nature sequencing paper found an overall slowing of base substitution rates in hominids compared to other primates and rodents, with chromosome 12 following this general trend
- **HOX cluster evolution**: The four-fold paralogy of HOX clusters (A, B, C, D) traces to two rounds of whole-genome duplication early in vertebrate evolution (~500 Mya). HOXC on chromosome 12 and its paralogous clusters preserve collinear gene order across 500+ Myr

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **HOX cluster chromatin topology**: HOXC genes are regulated by the Polycomb/Trithorax system with sharp H3K27me3/H3K4me3 boundaries ("bivalent domains" in stem cells). The cluster transitions from active to repressed chromatin states in a collinear fashion along the anterior-posterior axis, one of the clearest examples of how 1D genomic position maps to 3D chromatin architecture and body plan
- **12q13-15 amplicon structure**: In liposarcoma, the 12q13-15 amplicon forms supernumerary ring chromosomes and giant marker chromosomes. These aberrant structures are composed of massive tandem amplifications (up to hundreds of copies) of CDK4, MDM2, and HMGA2, creating some of the most architecturally extreme chromosome derivatives seen in human cancer
- **G-quadruplex in KRAS**: The KRAS promoter contains a well-characterized G-quadruplex structure (G4 motif) that acts as a transcriptional silencer. G4-stabilizing ligands suppress KRAS transcription -- an active area of drug development. The G4 structure is polymorphic, adopting parallel and antiparallel conformations depending on ionic conditions
- **Keratin cluster higher-order structure**: The 780 kb type II keratin domain at 12q13.13 is organized into a self-interacting topologically associating domain (TAD) with tissue-specific activation -- epidermal keratins are activated as a coordinated unit within this TAD
- **NANOG promoter architecture**: The NANOG promoter integrates inputs from OCT4, SOX2, STAT3, and NANOG itself (positive autoregulation) through a compact regulatory region. Enhancer elements show rapid evolutionary divergence between human and mouse despite functional conservation

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **HOX bivalent domains**: In embryonic stem cells, HOXC genes carry both H3K4me3 (activation) and H3K27me3 (repression) simultaneously -- the "bivalent domain" first described by Bernstein et al. (2006). During differentiation, bivalency resolves to monovalent active or repressed states in a position-dependent manner. HOXC is one of the largest bivalent domains in the genome
- **Replication timing**: The gene-dense 12q13 region replicates early in S-phase; the gene-poor 12p arm replicates late. The HOXC cluster shows cell-type-specific replication timing shifts that correlate with its activation state
- **CpG island methylation**: The HOXC cluster CpG islands are frequently hypermethylated in cancer (a Pan-cancer CIMP marker). HOXC9 and HOXC10 promoter methylation is prognostic in breast cancer. The keratin cluster CpG islands show tissue-specific methylation patterns corresponding to epithelial differentiation programs
- **KRAS epigenetic regulation**: KRAS expression is modulated by promoter methylation, the G4 structure, and multiple miRNAs (let-7 family). KRAS amplification in cancer can occur via chromothripsis events affecting 12p
- **NANOG epigenetic gating**: NANOG expression is tightly controlled by DNA methylation -- its promoter is heavily methylated in somatic cells (silencing) and demethylated in ESCs and germ cells. This methylation acts as an epigenetic gate preventing somatic cells from accessing the pluripotency network
- **Imprinted loci**: Chromosome 12 is relatively sparse in imprinted genes compared to chromosomes 7, 11, 14, and 15, though some evidence for imprinted loci exists at 12q

## Deep Cuts

- **The most drugged oncogene's long road**: KRAS was identified as an oncogene in 1982 and declared "undruggable" for nearly 40 years due to its picomolar GTP affinity and smooth surface. Sotorasib (AMG510), approved in 2021, was the first KRAS inhibitor -- covalently trapping the G12C mutant in its inactive state. The 39-year gap between target identification and approved drug is one of the longest in pharmaceutical history
- **Naming NANOG**: The gene was named by Austin Smith's lab after Tir na nOg, the Land of the Young in Celtic mythology -- a realm where nobody ages. The name reflects NANOG's function in maintaining the "forever young" state of stem cells. It has 11 pseudogenes scattered across the genome, more than almost any other transcription factor
- **The orangutan centromere experiment**: Chromosome 12 in orangutans provided the first definitive evidence that centromeres can reposition epigenetically. The orangutan centromere sits at a different position than in humans, with no underlying DNA sequence change -- proving that centromere identity is specified by CENP-A (an epigenetic histone variant), not DNA sequence. This overturned the "centromeric DNA determines centromere" hypothesis
- **GAPDH's double life**: The genome's most famous "boring" housekeeping gene is anything but. GAPDH moonlights in apoptosis (translocates to the nucleus upon S-nitrosylation), DNA repair, membrane fusion, and microtubule bundling. It is transcriptionally upregulated in many cancers and is now considered a poor choice as a normalization control -- yet remains the most commonly used reference gene in qPCR, a triumph of inertia over evidence
- **In utero origin of childhood leukemia**: The ETV6-RUNX1 fusion (from t(12;21)) can be detected in neonatal blood spots (Guthrie cards) of children who develop ALL years later -- proving the translocation occurs prenatally but requires additional postnatal hits. An estimated 1% of newborns may carry this fusion without ever developing leukemia, implying a >99% failure rate of this "first hit"
- **The ring chromosomes of liposarcoma**: Well-differentiated liposarcomas contain supernumerary ring chromosomes made almost entirely of amplified 12q13-15 material. These rings can contain hundreds of copies of CDK4 and MDM2. They are mitotically unstable, generating the most bizarre chromosome derivatives routinely seen in surgical pathology
- **Keratin gene archaeology**: The 26 type II keratin genes at 12q13 and 28 type I keratin genes at 17q21 arose from an ancient tandem duplication followed by chromosome fission. The paired keratin clusters are molecular fossils of an ancestral gene array that predates the mammalian radiation, with individual keratin genes traceable to specific duplication events >300 Mya
- **487 disease genes**: Chromosome 12 harbors 487 loci directly implicated in human disease -- placing it among the top 5 autosomes for disease gene density. This includes genes for PKU, Noonan syndrome, Stickler syndrome, von Willebrand disease type 2N, and dozens of skeletal dysplasias
