# Chromosome 10

> The tumor suppressor's home -- where PTEN guards the genome and RET wires the nervous system, all on a chromosome whose two arms were once separate chromosomes in our primate ancestors.

## Physical Properties

- **Total length**: ~133.8 Mb of euchromatic sequence (131.67 Mb finished in the 2004 Nature paper); represents 4.0--4.5% of total genomic DNA
- **Centromere type/position**: Submetacentric; centromere at ~39.7 Mb from p-terminus, yielding a short arm (10p) of ~39 Mb and a long arm (10q) of ~95 Mb
- **Cytogenetic group**: Group C (medium-sized submetacentric chromosomes, along with chr 6--12 and X)
- **GC content**: Overall ~41.0%, close to the genome average of 40.9%; pericentromeric 10p at 40.4% with peaks to 45--46% near satellite-3 arrays; 10q11 shows a dramatic gradient from 35% to 55% moving from centromeric satellites toward the RET locus
- **Gene density**: 10.4 genes/Mb (slightly below genome average of ~11 genes/Mb), ranking approximately 15th--17th among autosomes
- **Gene count**: 1,357 genes total (816 protein-coding, 430 pseudogenes, 67 antisense transcripts), spanning 66.3 Mb (about half the chromosome)

## Genomic Architecture

- **Isochore structure**: Predominantly L2/H1 isochores, with GC-rich H2/H3 patches concentrated in the distal 10q telomeric region, consistent with the chromosome's modest gene density
- **Pericentromeric complexity**: The sequences flanking the centromere are a "complex patchwork" of arm-specific sequences, stable duplications, and unstable repeats with homologies to telomeric and other centromeric locations. Two blocks of duplicated segments (~250 kb and ~150 kb) are separated by ~300 kb of unique DNA on each arm. Zinc-finger gene triads (ZNF11A/ZNF33A/ZNF37A on 10p; ZNF11B/ZNF33B/ZNF37B on 10q) are inverted across the centromere
- **Segmental duplications**: Inter- and intrachromosomal duplications have materially inflated the gene count; duplicated segments cluster in pericentromeric regions. 53% of evolutionary rearrangement breakpoints genome-wide associate with segmental duplications (vs. 18% expected by chance)
- **Common fragile sites**: Two notable fragile sites:
  - **FRA10A** (10q23.3) -- folate-sensitive, caused by CGG repeat expansion (>200 repeats trigger CpG methylation) in the FRA10AC1 gene encoding a conserved nuclear protein
  - **FRA10B** (10q25.2) -- BrdU-inducible, caused by expansion of a ~42 bp AT-rich minisatellite. Unusually, FRA10B does not replicate late -- a striking exception to the "late replication" model of fragile site expression
- **Repeat content**: Typical LINE/SINE representation; no exceptional Alu enrichment or depletion reported relative to similarly sized chromosomes

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **PTEN** (10q23.31) -- Phosphatase and Tensin Homolog. The second most frequently mutated tumor suppressor in human cancer (after TP53). Dual-specificity phosphatase that antagonizes the PI3K/AKT pathway; germline mutations cause Cowden syndrome (lifetime breast cancer risk ~85%), Bannayan-Riley-Ruvalcaba syndrome, and Lhermitte-Duclos disease. Somatically deleted or mutated in glioblastoma (~40%), endometrial carcinoma (~50%), prostate cancer, and melanoma
- **RET** (10q11.21) -- Rearranged during Transfection proto-oncogene. Receptor tyrosine kinase essential for neural crest development and kidney morphogenesis. Gain-of-function mutations cause MEN2A/2B and familial medullary thyroid carcinoma; loss-of-function mutations cause Hirschsprung disease (aganglionic megacolon). The same gene thus causes both cancer and a developmental defect -- a rare biological duality
- **FGFR2** (10q26.13) -- Fibroblast Growth Factor Receptor 2. Mutations cause craniosynostosis syndromes (Crouzon, Pfeiffer, Apert, Jackson-Weiss). One of the most frequently mutated genes in endometrial cancer
- **ALOX5** (10q11.21) -- Arachidonate 5-Lipoxygenase. Converts arachidonic acid to leukotrienes; key inflammatory mediator; maintains cancer stem cells in leukemia
- **GATA3** (10p14) -- Master regulator of T-cell development and mammary gland morphogenesis. Haploinsufficiency causes HDR syndrome (hypoparathyroidism, deafness, renal anomalies). One of the most frequently mutated genes in breast cancer (~10% of luminal subtypes)
- **SUFU** (10q24.32) -- Suppressor of Fused. Negative regulator of Hedgehog signaling; biallelic loss causes medulloblastoma and Joubert syndrome
- **VIM** (10p13) -- Vimentin. Type III intermediate filament protein; the canonical mesenchymal marker; its upregulation defines the epithelial-mesenchymal transition in cancer
- **Gene clusters**: Olfactory receptor cluster at 10p (smaller than chr 11's massive OR complement); zinc-finger gene paralogons flanking centromere
- **Largest genes**: CTNNA3 at 10q21 spans ~1.8 Mb, one of the largest genes in the human genome

## Disease Associations

- **10p deletion syndrome**: Terminal deletions of 10p cause two distinct phenotypes depending on breakpoint location:
  - **DiGeorge syndrome type 2 (DGS2)** -- more proximal deletions at 10p13-14: cardiac defects, thymic hypoplasia, T-cell deficiency (phenocopy of classic 22q11.2 deletion)
  - **HDR syndrome** -- more distal deletions encompassing GATA3 at 10p14: hypoparathyroidism, sensorineural deafness, renal dysplasia
- **Cancer hotspots**: 10q23 (PTEN) is among the most frequently deleted regions across all human cancers; 10q11 (RET) rearrangements drive ~20% of papillary thyroid carcinomas (RET/PTC fusions)
- **GWAS loci**: Susceptibility loci for Alzheimer disease (IDE/HHEX region at 10q), type 1 diabetes, schizophrenia, and restless legs syndrome (BTBD16/MEIS1 region) map to chromosome 10
- **Structural variants**: Recurrent inv(10)(p11.2q21.2) in papillary thyroid carcinoma generates the RET/PTC1 fusion; t(10;14)(q24;q11) in T-cell ALL juxtaposes HOX11 to TCR-alpha
- **Disease gene burden**: 85 of the 816 protein-coding genes are associated with >100 genetic diseases, plus susceptibility loci for 17 common multifactorial diseases

## Evolutionary History

- **Ancestral origin**: Human chromosome 10 derives from the fusion of two ancestral primate chromosomes. In prosimian ancestors, 10p and 10q existed as separate chromosomes (designated PHYL-10p and PHYL-10q). These fused into a single chromosome in the common ancestor of Old World monkeys and apes
- **New World monkey configuration**: In platyrrhines (New World monkeys), PHYL-10p remained a separate chromosome, while PHYL-10q was associated with material homologous to human chromosome 16
- **Orangutan form**: In the ancestor of great apes, chromosome 10 achieved its current configuration with marker order corresponding to the orangutan ortholog
- **Mouse synteny**: Mouse chromosome 10 (MMU10) contains regions syntenic to human chromosomes 10, 21, and 22 -- the junction between HSA21 and HSA22 homologous material on MMU10 has been narrowed to an 18 kb interval
- **Chimpanzee**: Human and chimpanzee chromosome 10 orthologs are largely collinear; no major inversions or rearrangements distinguish them
- **Deep conservation**: 13 segments of conserved synteny with chicken (>300 Myr divergence) cover ~72% of the chromosome, indicating strong structural conservation across vertebrates

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **G-quadruplex landscape**: The PTEN promoter region is rich in G-quadruplex-forming sequences; G4 structures in the PTEN 5'UTR regulate translational efficiency and are therapeutic targets for G4-stabilizing small molecules in cancer
- **Fragile site biophysics**: FRA10B's AT-rich minisatellite forms unusual secondary structures (cruciform/hairpin DNA) that trigger chromosomal breakage; notably, its replication timing is NOT delayed, contradicting the canonical model of fragile-site pathogenesis
- **Pericentromeric compositional boundary**: The 10q11 pericentromeric region transitions from 35% to 55% GC across just ~2 Mb -- one of the steepest compositional gradients on any chromosome, possibly a molecular fossil of the ancestral chromosome fusion
- **Telomeric/subtelomeric features**: 10p subtelomere contains segmental duplications shared with multiple other chromosomes, participating in the dynamic subtelomeric exchange network that drives primate genome evolution
- **CTNNA3 ultralong gene**: At ~1.8 Mb, CTNNA3 is among the largest genes in the genome; its enormous introns may harbor long-range regulatory elements and common fragile site-like features

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **Imprinted regions**: Chromosome 10 is relatively sparse in imprinted loci compared to chromosomes 7, 11, 14, and 15. No major imprinting control regions comparable to IGF2/H19 or PWS/AS have been identified on this chromosome
- **Replication timing**: The gene-poor pericentromeric region and central 10q gene desert replicate late in S-phase; distal 10q26 (FGFR2 region) replicates early, consistent with its GC-rich, gene-dense character
- **PTEN epigenetic regulation**: PTEN is subject to extensive epigenetic silencing in cancer via promoter hypermethylation, a mechanism functionally equivalent to genetic deletion. Its pseudogene PTENP1 (on chr 9) acts as a ceRNA sponge for PTEN-targeting miRNAs -- a cross-chromosomal epigenetic regulatory axis
- **Chromatin states**: The FRA10A CGG expansion triggers heterochromatic spreading with H3K9me3 and DNA methylation -- mechanistically analogous to Fragile X (FMR1) on the X chromosome
- **DNA methylation valleys**: The RET promoter sits within a DNA methylation valley (DMV), a broad hypomethylated domain characteristic of developmental master regulators

## Deep Cuts

- **The dual-personality gene**: RET is one of very few genes where activating mutations cause cancer (MEN2) and inactivating mutations cause a developmental disorder (Hirschsprung disease). The same cysteine residues in the extracellular domain can either constitutively dimerize the receptor (cancer) or prevent its folding (aganglionosis)
- **DiGeorge phenocopy**: DGS2 on 10p is clinically indistinguishable from classic DiGeorge syndrome (22q11.2 deletion) -- the two chromosomes converge on the same pharyngeal arch/neural crest developmental pathway, suggesting a shared regulatory network spanning different chromosomes
- **FRA10B breaks the rules**: Most fragile sites break because they replicate late; FRA10B breaks despite normal replication timing, driven purely by its AT-rich secondary structure. It is the key counterexample to the late-replication model of chromosomal fragility
- **PTEN's bodyguard**: PTENP1, a processed pseudogene on chromosome 9, protects PTEN mRNA from miRNA-mediated degradation by acting as a competitive endogenous RNA. Deletion of this pseudogene is tumorigenic even though it produces no protein -- arguably the most important "dead gene" in oncology
- **Two arms, two chromosomes**: The fact that 10p and 10q were separate chromosomes in early primates means every human carries a fused chromosome at this locus. The pericentromeric zinc-finger paralogons (ZNF triads) are molecular fossils of this ancient fusion event
- **The steepest GC cliff**: The 10q11 compositional transition from 35% to 55% GC across ~2 Mb is one of the most dramatic isochore boundaries in the genome -- a physical signature of the ancestral fusion event that still shapes local chromatin architecture
- **67 antisense transcripts**: The 2004 sequencing paper identified 67 antisense transcripts overlapping protein-coding genes -- widespread bidirectional transcription on a chromosome once thought to have "average" complexity
