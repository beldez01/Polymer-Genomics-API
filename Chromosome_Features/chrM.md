# Mitochondrial Genome

> *A bacterial genome trapped inside us for two billion years, running our cells with just 37 genes, no introns, and a genetic code all its own.*

## Physical Properties

- **Structure**: Circular, double-stranded DNA; 16,569 bp (Cambridge Reference Sequence).
- **Copies per cell**: ~1,000-10,000 depending on tissue. Mature oocytes: >150,000 copies. Sperm: ~50-75.
- **No centromere, no telomeres**: Circular molecule replicated by mitochondrial DNA polymerase gamma (POLG).
- **Total genes**: 37 -- the most compact functional genome in the human body: 13 protein-coding (all OXPHOS subunits), 22 tRNAs, 2 rRNAs.
- **Gene density**: 93.5% coding sequence. Essentially no intergenic spacers except the D-loop (~1,122 bp).
- **Mass contribution**: 0.0005% of genomic DNA by sequence, but ~1% of total cellular DNA by mass due to high copy number.

## Genomic Architecture

- **Heavy (H) and Light (L) strands**: Named for buoyant density. H-strand encodes 12/13 mRNAs, both rRNAs, 14/22 tRNAs. L-strand encodes ND6 and 8 tRNAs.
- **D-loop (Displacement loop)**: ~1.1 kb non-coding control region containing origins of replication, promoters, and three conserved sequence blocks. The hypervariable segment HVS-I is used in forensic identification.
- **No introns**: Every gene is intronless -- bacterial ancestry.
- **Overlapping reading frames**: ATP8/ATP6 overlap by 46 nucleotides. ND4L/ND4 overlap by 7. Same DNA encodes parts of two proteins in different frames.
- **tRNA punctuation model** (Ojala, Montoya & Attardi, 1981): Entire H-strand transcribed as one polycistronic transcript. tRNA folding serves as the processing signal -- one of the most elegant gene expression strategies in biology.
- **Strand-asymmetric replication**: H-strand from OH; L-strand origin (OL) ~11,000 bp away. H-strand synthesis proceeds ~2/3 around before L-strand initiates.

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **MT-ND1 through MT-ND6 + MT-ND4L**: Seven Complex I subunits. Complex I mutations are the most common cause of mitochondrial disease.
- **MT-CO1, MT-CO2, MT-CO3**: Three Complex IV subunits. MT-CO1 is the universal DNA barcode for animal species identification.
- **MT-CYB**: Only mtDNA-encoded Complex III subunit. Target of antimalarial atovaquone.
- **MT-ATP6, MT-ATP8**: Two Complex V subunits. m.8993T>G causes NARP syndrome and Leigh syndrome.
- **MT-RNR1 (12S rRNA)**: m.1555A>G causes aminoglycoside-induced deafness (~1/500 people). A single dose of gentamicin can cause irreversible bilateral hearing loss in carriers.
- **MT-TL1 (tRNALeu)**: m.3243A>G responsible for ~80% of MELAS. Most common pathogenic mtDNA variant overall (~1/400 carrier frequency).
- **D-loop/Control Region**: Fastest-evolving region, ~10x nuclear genome rate. Invaluable for forensics and population genetics.

## Disease Associations

- **MELAS**: m.3243A>G in MT-TL1. Stroke-like episodes ages 20-40. Same mutation causes MIDD (diabetes and deafness) at lower heteroplasmy.
- **MERRF**: m.8344A>G in MT-TK. "Ragged red fibers" on muscle biopsy.
- **LHON**: Three primary mutations account for ~95%. Sudden bilateral vision loss, 4:1 male:female despite maternal inheritance.
- **Heteroplasmy threshold effect**: Disease manifests only when mutant load exceeds tissue-specific threshold: ~60% for biochemical defects, ~80-90% for clinical disease.
- **Leigh syndrome**: Most common mitochondrial disease of childhood.
- **Common deletion (4,977 bp)**: Causes Pearson syndrome (sideroblastic anemia) in infancy; survivors develop Kearns-Sayre syndrome.

## Evolutionary History

- **Endosymbiosis**: ~1.5-2 billion years ago, alpha-proteobacterium engulfed by archaeal host. Most consequential merger in the history of life.
- **99% gene transfer**: Ancestral 3,000-5,000 gene genome reduced to 37. Remaining 13 protein-coding genes hypothesized to stay because hydrophobic membrane proteins cannot be imported post-translationally.
- **NUMTs**: Over 700 mtDNA fragments transferred to nuclear genome, totaling ~600 kb. Serve as "molecular fossils." Can confound sequencing.
- **Mitochondrial Eve**: All mtDNA lineages coalesce to a single woman ~150,000-200,000 years ago in Africa. Not the only woman alive -- just the most recent common matrilineal ancestor.
- **Non-universal genetic code**: UGA = Trp (not Stop), AGA/AGG = Stop (not Arg), AUA = Met (not Ile). Evidence of independent evolutionary trajectory.
- **Clock-like evolution**: ~10-17x faster than nuclear DNA due to ROS proximity, limited repair, no protective histones, no recombination.

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **Nucleoid structure**: Packaged by TFAM (HMG-box protein) that bends DNA ~180 degrees, compacting molecules into ~100 nm nucleoid particles with typically 1-2 mtDNA molecules each.
- **G-quadruplex at CSB II**: Hybrid RNA-DNA G4 during transcription causes premature termination, generating the H-strand replication primer. Conserved across vertebrates.
- **Supercoiling**: Exists in supercoiled, relaxed circular, and linear forms. TFAM levels modulate supercoiling state.
- **No histones**: TFAM is the sole packaging protein. No regular repeating structure like chromatin.
- **D-loop R-loop**: Persistent RNA-DNA hybrid -- one of the longest-lived R-loops in biology.

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **mtDNA methylation debate**: 5mC and 5hmC detected; DNMT isoforms found in mitochondria. Biological significance remains contested.
- **6-methyladenine**: N6-methyladenine detected -- a bacterial-like modification reflecting prokaryotic ancestry.
- **The mitochondrial bottleneck**: Copy number drops from ~150,000 in oocyte to ~200 in primordial germ cells, then re-expands. Amplifies heteroplasmy variation between offspring.
- **Retrograde signaling**: Mitochondrial metabolites (acetyl-CoA, alpha-ketoglutarate, NAD+, SAM) are essential cofactors for nuclear chromatin-modifying enzymes. mtDNA mutations can alter nuclear epigenetic patterns.

## Deep Cuts

- **97% of mitochondrial proteins are nuclear-encoded**: Only 13 of ~1,500 mitochondrial proteins come from mtDNA. The remaining ~1,487 are imported from the cytoplasm.
- **Paternal mtDNA is actively destroyed**: Sperm mitochondria are tagged with ubiquitin and degraded by mitophagy. Active elimination, not passive dilution.
- **The pharmacogenomic time bomb**: m.1555A>G (carried by ~1/500 people) creates a ribosome resembling the bacterial aminoglycoside target. One injection of gentamicin = permanent bilateral deafness.
- **MT-CO1 as DNA barcode**: Ironically, a gene that has been in mtDNA for 2 billion years is now used to classify the diversity of organisms it inhabits.
- **Mitochondrial Eve misconception**: Not the first woman, not the only woman -- just the most recent common matrilineal ancestor. Many contemporaries have living descendants through at least one male link.
- **The D-loop replication puzzle**: ~95% of replication events initiated at OH are aborted after ~650 nt (producing 7S DNA). Why the cell aborts most attempts remains unknown.
- **Two genomes, one respiratory chain**: Complexes I, III, IV, and V each contain subunits from both mtDNA and nuclear DNA -- coordinated expression from two genomes with different genetic codes, different replication, different inheritance. A tightrope walk maintained for 2 billion years.
- **mtDNA copy number as biomarker**: Low copy number in blood associated with increased CVD, diabetes, and mortality risk. Being investigated as an inexpensive aging biomarker from any blood sample.
