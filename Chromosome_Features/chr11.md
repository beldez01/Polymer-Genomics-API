# Chromosome 11

> The genome's most gene-dense autosome and birthplace of the olfactory receptor superfamily -- home to the beta-globin cluster that taught us gene regulation, the IGF2/H19 imprinting paradigm, and more disease genes per megabase than almost any other chromosome.

## Physical Properties

- **Total length**: ~134.5 Mb (99.8% euchromatic coverage in the 2006 Nature sequencing paper); represents ~4.5% of total genomic DNA
- **Centromere type/position**: Submetacentric; centromere near position ~53.4 Mb, yielding a short arm (11p) of ~53 Mb and a long arm (11q) of ~82 Mb
- **Cytogenetic group**: Group C (medium-sized submetacentric chromosomes)
- **GC content**: ~41.6%, slightly above genome average, but with extreme local variation -- the beta-globin LCR region is AT-rich while the 11p15.5 imprinted domain is GC-rich
- **Gene density**: 11.6 genes/Mb -- one of the highest among all autosomes, ranking in the top 5. Contains 1,524 protein-coding genes and 765 pseudogenes
- **Gene count**: ~2,300 total annotated genes. The Broad Institute described it as the chromosome that "rolls high number" for its outsized gene and disease content

## Genomic Architecture

- **Olfactory receptor (OR) megacluster**: 369 of the genome's 856 OR genes (43%) reside on chromosome 11, distributed across 28 single- and multi-gene clusters. This is by far the richest OR chromosome -- ORs represent >10% of all chromosome 11 genes. Two clusters exceed 100 OR genes each, the largest in the genome. The OR4 superfamily cluster (48.1--55.2 Mb) was fractured into four pieces by centromere insertion, a heterochromatic block, and an intrachromosomal duplication. Chromosome 11 is the only chromosome containing Class I olfactory receptors and has 9 of 13 Class II families -- strong evidence that it was the evolutionary birthplace of the OR repertoire
- **Isochore structure**: Heterogeneous -- 11p15 is GC-rich and gene-dense; central 11q contains large L1/L2 isochore domains; distal 11q is moderately GC-rich
- **Segmental duplications**: Pericentromeric region heavily duplicated; OR gene clusters themselves represent massive tandem and interspersed segmental duplications, with evidence of ongoing copy-number variation
- **Common fragile sites**: FRA11B (11q23.3) maps to the CBL2 locus; FRA11A is folate-sensitive at 11q13.3. The 11q23 region is a recurrent breakpoint zone in leukemia
- **Repeat content**: The OR gene clusters are interspersed with LINE elements that may have facilitated their tandem expansion; Alu density is elevated in the gene-dense 11p15 region

## Notable Genes & Loci

- **Beta-globin gene cluster** (11p15.4) -- Five genes arranged 5'-epsilon-Ggamma-Agamma-delta-beta-3' spanning ~70 kb, controlled by the Locus Control Region (LCR), a 34 kb super-enhancer upstream of epsilon-globin. The LCR was the first identified long-range cis-regulatory element. Genes are activated sequentially during development (embryonic epsilon -> fetal gamma -> adult delta/beta), with position relative to the LCR dictating temporal expression. Deletion of the LCR silences the entire 70 kb domain into heterochromatin even though the genes themselves are intact -- a landmark demonstration that regulatory elements can control chromatin state across tens of kilobases
- **IGF2/H19 imprinting domain** (11p15.5) -- The paradigmatic imprinted locus. IGF2 (paternally expressed growth factor) and H19 (maternally expressed long noncoding RNA) are 90 kb apart, share a 3' enhancer, and are regulated by ICR1, a differentially methylated region with 7 CTCF-binding sites. On the maternal allele, unmethylated ICR1 binds CTCF, forming a chromatin insulator that blocks IGF2 from the enhancer. On the paternal allele, ICR1 is methylated, CTCF cannot bind, and IGF2 is activated while H19 is silenced
- **WT1** (11p13) -- Wilms Tumor 1. Zinc-finger transcription factor essential for kidney and gonad development. Mutations cause Wilms tumor, WAGR syndrome (Wilms tumor, aniridia, genitourinary anomalies, retardation), Denys-Drash syndrome, and Frasier syndrome. Alternative splicing generates +/-KTS isoforms with distinct functions in transcription vs. RNA processing
- **INS** (11p15.5) -- Insulin gene. Physically contiguous with tyrosine hydroxylase (TH), separated by only 2.7 kb of flanking DNA. Both genes have the same transcriptional polarity in a head-to-tail arrangement: 5'-TH-INS-IGF2-3'. The INS VNTR (variable number tandem repeat) upstream of the gene influences transcription levels and is a type 1 diabetes susceptibility locus
- **ATM** (11q22.3) -- Ataxia Telangiectasia Mutated. Serine/threonine kinase that is a master regulator of the DNA damage response. Biallelic loss causes ataxia-telangiectasia (cerebellar degeneration, immunodeficiency, cancer predisposition, radiation hypersensitivity). Heterozygous carriers have ~2x breast cancer risk
- **MEN1** (11q13) -- Menin 1. Nuclear scaffold protein; germline mutations cause Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia type 1 (parathyroid adenomas, pituitary tumors, pancreatic islet cell tumors). The gene spans 10 exons encoding a 610-amino acid protein
- **HRAS** (11p15.5) -- Harvey Ras oncogene. One of the first oncogenes discovered; Costello syndrome results from germline activating mutations
- **Catalase (CAT)** (11p13) -- The major hydrogen peroxide detoxification enzyme; adjacent to WT1, deletion of both causes WAGR syndrome features

## Disease Associations

- **Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (BWS)**: Caused by epigenetic or genetic alterations at 11p15.5 affecting two imprinted domains -- ICR1 (IGF2/H19) and ICR2 (KCNQ1/CDKN1C). Gain of methylation at ICR1 activates biallelic IGF2 expression; loss of methylation at ICR2 silences CDKN1C. Overgrowth, macroglossia, organomegaly, embryonal tumors (especially Wilms tumor and hepatoblastoma)
- **Silver-Russell syndrome**: The mirror image of BWS -- loss of methylation at ICR1 leads to biallelic H19 expression and IGF2 silencing. Growth restriction, body asymmetry, characteristic facial features
- **Sickle cell disease and beta-thalassemia**: Point mutations (sickle: Glu6Val in HBB) or deletions/mutations reducing beta-globin output. Sickle cell disease was the first human disease understood at the molecular level (Pauling 1949, Ingram 1956)
- **Jacobsen syndrome (11q terminal deletion)**: Deletion of 5--16 Mb from 11q24.1 to the telomere. Features include thrombocytopenia (Paris-Trousseau syndrome from FLI1 haploinsufficiency), cardiac defects, intellectual disability. Prevalence <1/100,000
- **Wilms tumor**: WT1 mutations at 11p13 plus LOH/LOI at 11p15 (IGF2/H19). Genetic and epigenetic alterations on 11p are involved in the majority of sporadic Wilms tumors
- **Cancer translocations**: t(11;14)(q13;q32) in mantle cell lymphoma juxtaposes CCND1 to IGH; 11q23 rearrangements involving MLL/KMT2A are hallmarks of infant leukemia and therapy-related AML
- **GWAS hits**: Type 1 diabetes (INS VNTR), type 2 diabetes (KCNQ1), breast cancer (ATM, CCND1), coronary artery disease

## Evolutionary History

- **Ancestral origin**: Chromosome 11 corresponds largely to a single ancestral mammalian chromosome that has been conserved as a syntenic block across placental mammals. It shows extensive synteny with mouse chromosome 7 (proximal and distal regions) and mouse chromosome 2
- **OR gene origin**: The concentration of both Class I and Class II olfactory receptors -- with 9 of 13 Class II families represented -- strongly supports chromosome 11 as the evolutionary birthplace of the vertebrate OR repertoire. The OR4 superfamily cluster predates the centromere insertion that now splits it
- **Centromere invasion**: The current centromere position disrupted the ancient OR4 cluster, suggesting centromere repositioning (neocentromere formation or centromere shift) occurred after the OR gene expansion
- **Primate conservation**: Human and chimpanzee chromosome 11 orthologs are highly collinear. Pericentric inversions distinguish some primate lineages but the overall structure is conserved across great apes
- **Deep conservation of globin cluster**: The beta-globin gene cluster organization (5'-embryonic-fetal-adult-3') is conserved across all amniotes, reflecting >300 Myr of maintained synteny and regulatory architecture

## Biophysical & Structural Features

- **LCR chromatin looping**: The beta-globin LCR engages in developmentally regulated chromatin loops, forming an "active chromatin hub" that brings the LCR into physical contact with the expressed globin gene while the intervening genes loop out. This was among the first demonstrations of long-range chromatin looping visualized by 3C/4C technology
- **CTCF insulation at IGF2/H19**: The 7 CTCF-binding sites in ICR1 create a methylation-sensitive chromatin insulator -- one of the best-characterized examples of how a structural protein reads DNA methylation to partition a chromatin domain into independently regulated compartments
- **Olfactory receptor chromatin**: OR genes use a remarkable "one receptor per neuron" expression mechanism involving stochastic choice, heterochromatic silencing of all non-chosen alleles, and interchromosomal enhancer sharing with a single trans-acting super-enhancer (the Greek Islands). The 369 OR genes on chr 11 participate in this genome-wide competitive silencing network
- **G-quadruplex enrichment**: The GC-rich 11p15.5 region (encompassing HRAS, INS, IGF2) is enriched for G-quadruplex-forming sequences. The HRAS promoter G4 structure has been extensively characterized biophysically and is a model system for G4-mediated transcriptional regulation
- **11q23 breakpoint fragility**: The MLL/KMT2A locus at 11q23 contains an 8.3 kb breakpoint cluster region enriched in scaffold attachment regions (SARs/MARs) and topoisomerase II cleavage sites, explaining its vulnerability to recombination during apoptotic DNA fragmentation (the "apoptotic nuclease" model of therapy-related leukemia)

## Epigenetic Landscape

- **Premier imprinting chromosome**: Chromosome 11p15.5 contains two of the best-characterized imprinting control regions in the genome:
  - **ICR1** (H19/IGF2 domain): Paternally methylated DMR with 7 CTCF sites; controls reciprocal expression of IGF2 and H19
  - **ICR2** (KCNQ1OT1/CDKN1C domain): Maternally methylated DMR controlling expression of KCNQ1, CDKN1C, and other imprinted genes via the KCNQ1OT1 antisense RNA
- **LCR as chromatin organizer**: Deletion of the beta-globin LCR converts the entire 70 kb domain to heterochromatin, demonstrating that enhancers can function as anti-silencing elements maintaining euchromatic state
- **Replication timing**: The IGF2/H19 region shows parent-of-origin-specific replication asynchrony -- the paternal allele replicates earlier than the maternal allele, one of the hallmarks of imprinted domains
- **DNA methylation**: The INS promoter methylation is tissue-specific and age-dependent; circulating unmethylated INS DNA in blood serves as a biomarker for beta-cell destruction in type 1 diabetes
- **Histone modification patterns**: The LCR contains DNase I hypersensitive sites (HS1-HS5) marked by H3K4me3 and H3K27ac in erythroid cells; non-erythroid tissues show H3K27me3 Polycomb silencing across the globin domain

## Deep Cuts

- **The first molecular disease**: Sickle cell disease (HBB Glu6Val) was identified as a "molecular disease" by Linus Pauling in 1949, then pinpointed to a single amino acid by Vernon Ingram in 1956. This was the founding event of molecular medicine, and it happened on chromosome 11
- **The 2.7 kb gap**: Tyrosine hydroxylase (TH) and insulin (INS) are separated by only 2.7 kb of DNA on 11p15.5. The rate-limiting enzyme for catecholamine synthesis sits essentially on top of the hormone that regulates glucose metabolism -- a genomic coincidence with no known functional coupling, but a striking example of how gene order can be evolutionarily frozen despite functional divergence
- **Olfactory receptor graveyard**: Of the 369 OR genes on chromosome 11, a large fraction are pseudogenes -- "dead" olfactory receptors inactivated during primate evolution as the visual system expanded and olfaction became less critical. Chromosome 11 contains the evolutionary graveyard of our lost sense of smell
- **Insulator as disease mechanism**: BWS and Silver-Russell syndrome are mirror-image diseases caused by opposite epimutations at the same CTCF-dependent insulator (ICR1). Gain of methylation = IGF2 overexpression = overgrowth (BWS). Loss of methylation = IGF2 silencing = growth restriction (SRS). The insulator is the disease
- **The LCR's kill switch**: Naturally occurring ~30 kb deletions of the beta-globin LCR silence the structurally intact HBB gene 60 kb downstream -- causing beta-thalassemia from a deletion that does not touch the gene itself. This was the experiment of nature that proved enhancers can control chromatin state at extreme distances
- **Chromosome 11 rolls high**: The Broad Institute's own description noted that chromosome 11 is disproportionately loaded with disease genes relative to its size. It harbors >487 OMIM disease loci, ranking among the top 3 autosomes for disease gene density
- **Paris-Trousseau meets Jacobsen**: The thrombocytopenia in Jacobsen syndrome (11q deletion) is caused by haploinsufficiency of FLI1, an ETS-family transcription factor required for megakaryocyte maturation. The giant alpha-granules seen in platelets of these patients are pathognomonic and were originally described as a separate entity (Paris-Trousseau syndrome) before the shared genetic basis was recognized
